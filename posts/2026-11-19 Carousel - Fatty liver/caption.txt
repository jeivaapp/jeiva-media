Your liver works quietly, around the clock, and rarely complains. That's exactly why fatty liver is so easy to miss.

Fatty liver means extra fat has built up in liver cells. Doctors are increasingly calling the non-alcohol form MASLD, a newer name that reflects its close link with blood sugar, weight around the waist and cholesterol. It's not about drinking, and it's not only found in people who look overweight.

Across Indian studies, rates were highest in people already living with diabetes, obesity or heart conditions, which is why doctors often check the liver when they see those. In studies drawn from the general community, it was still found in more than 1 in 4 adults.

Here's the encouraging part. The liver is remarkably good at recovering. US liver specialists' guidance notes that even modest, steady weight loss can reduce liver fat, and regular activity helps on its own, even before the scale moves. Cutting back on sugary drinks and highly processed foods supports both.

How is it found? Often by chance: a routine ultrasound or a liver function test done for another reason. If you have diabetes, prediabetes or carry weight around your middle, it's reasonable to ask your doctor whether checking your liver makes sense.

Look after the organ that looks after you.
